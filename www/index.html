Can Chicago reach its 2040 goal?

The City's 2022 Climate Action Plan aims to cut citywide emissions 62% below 2017 levels by 2040.1 Heating, cooling and powering buildings and industry caused 69% of Chicago's emissions in 2017. Adjust the settings below to see how changes in energy efficiency, electrification, and cleaning the electric grid can decrease Chicago's emissions over time.

Notes/Sources

How buildings are grouped

  • Residential buildings are split in two. Multifamily housing is the large buildings in the benchmarking data that are places to live: apartment buildings, dormitories, senior care and residential care communities, and other lodging or residential buildings. Other homes and small buildings is everything else the City counts as residential, mostly single-family homes, two-flats and small apartment buildings. It is estimated using the remainder from citywide measurements of total building emissions.
  • Commercial buildings are the other benchmarked types (offices, hospitals, schools, hotels, etc.) plus a remainder for smaller commercial buildings. The City reports water pumping and wastewater treatment energy in this group too, so the remainder includes a portion of that.
  • Industry (manufacturing and construction) is not in the benchmarking data. Its energy is calculated from the 2017 citywide emissions data.
